## Overview

At Incentiva Group, I led ChatFlow, the company's platform for WhatsApp AI assistants, and on it the WhatsApp assistant of Colmaíz, Ingredion's brand. ChatFlow is a React panel, a Node API on Azure, Claude models and the WhatsApp Cloud API. One platform runs the assistants of several client lines, from Colmaíz and the [Samsung × LifeMiles promotion](/work/samsung-lifemiles-whatsapp) I led at Incentiva Group to the sales line of Incentiva as an authorized DIRECTV dealer, so a change in one place reaches every client at once.

When I took it on, ChatFlow lived as backups: there was no repository yet, deploys were zip uploads and there were no tests. That was the starting point. The platform was serving real conversations every day, and Colmaíz's team did not yet have a view of its own conversations.

**Result: a platform under version control, behind a gated pipeline, audited three times and covered by 3,177 automated tests, with a client portal that Colmaíz's commercial team uses daily.**

## The Build

### From backups to a repository

The first job was knowing what was actually running. I imported the code into git and restored parity with production, which meant recovering files that existed only on the server and nowhere in the backups. From that point the repository was the truth, and every change had a history.

### A pipeline with gates

Deploys moved from zip uploads to a CI/CD pipeline that builds and tests every change, while production deploys wait behind manual approval gates, with a documented deploy recipe so a release does not depend on memory. A set of guard tests fails the build on secret leaks, unsafe calls or routes mounted without protection.

### Three audits, then the fixes

I ran three audits: a multi-tenant risk audit, a full code audit and a security audit. Together they raised more than 100 findings. They were prioritized, fixed and deployed in releases, and the security audit's first remediation release came with a rotation of the platform's secrets.

### Seeing the platform run

Observability came next: metrics and alerts, AI consumption tracked per client and per campaign, and personal data masked in telemetry, so the logs that help debug a conversation do not become a copy of it.

### Features the operation needed

With the base stable, the platform grew where the operation needed it: message delivery states, a WhatsApp templates interface and quick replies, a metrics dashboard with Excel export and an audit module, reliable broadcasts with deduplication, retries, atomic claims and local time zones, and a contact identity for WhatsApp users who do not show a phone number.

### A portal for the client

Colmaíz's team needed to see and act on its own conversations, so I led a client-role portal on top of the platform. The client role is isolated in every layer, from the interface to the API to the data. It gives Colmaíz's commercial team a dashboard with Excel export, read-only live chats, location KPIs that map each city to its department and its responsible technician, salesperson or distributor, and "who to call" lists. Before release, **643 dashboard figures were reconciled three ways**, against the API, the Excel export and SQL.

The Colmaíz assistant itself got sharper too. An audit of its answers against its sources led to knowledge loaded per turn by context, the assistant moved to a faster, lower-cost model, and it now gives a contact instead of promising a handoff it cannot make.

The work was built with AI coding agents (Claude Code) under my direction. I ran the takeover, decided what each audit meant for the roadmap, approved the releases and followed up the incidents.

## Results

- The repository grew from **139 files and no tests to 831 files and 3,177 passing tests**.
- **684 commits and more than 200 merged pull requests**.
- **Three audits** with more than 100 findings, fixed and deployed in releases.
- The assistant's test battery improved from **270 to 318 of 320**, and **643 dashboard figures** were reconciled three ways before the portal shipped.
- The Colmaíz assistant serves a community of **35,000+ contacts**, with **6,000+ served**.
- The same platform ran the [Samsung × LifeMiles promotion](/work/samsung-lifemiles-whatsapp), whose chatbot awarded buyers **2M+ LifeMiles miles**.

## What This Demonstrates

Taking over a running system is a different job from building one. You cannot stop it, you first have to learn exactly what is running, and every change has to land without breaking the conversations happening right now. The order mattered: first the truth (a repository that matches production), then a safe way to change it (gates and tests), then the audits, and only then new features.

The 3,177 tests are not the goal in themselves. They are what lets a small team change a multi-client platform with confidence that no client will notice.
