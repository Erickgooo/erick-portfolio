## Overview

At Incentiva Group, I led the Samsung × LifeMiles campaign. Buyers of a Galaxy from the S26 family received LifeMiles miles for their purchase, and they claimed them through a WhatsApp chatbot running on [ChatFlow](/work/chatflow-whatsapp-ai-platform), Incentiva's own platform for WhatsApp AI assistants.

The idea is simple for the buyer: open WhatsApp, send a photo of the invoice and the phone's IMEI, confirm who you are, and the miles arrive. Behind it, the assistant has to read invoices of every kind, check that the phone is real and eligible, confirm the buyer's identity and credit the miles, and it has to do that while some of the people writing to it are trying to claim miles they are not owed. A promotion that pays in something of real value attracts exactly that.

The assistant was already live when the campaign came to me. My job was to take it to the level a high-value, high-volume promotion asks for: invoice validation that reads any invoice, closing summaries built only on verified data, customer messages with nothing internal in them, and decisions that a persuasive message cannot reach.

**Result: more than 2 million LifeMiles miles were awarded to Samsung buyers through the chatbot, with the fraud controls live before the promotion's peak.**

## The Build

### Leading the campaign

My first job was the campaign itself, from Incentiva's side: owning the promotion's rules on the platform, deciding what the assistant needed before the peak and in what order, and being accountable for every mile it credited. That order mattered. Fraud controls and validation came before anything cosmetic, because every claim was worth real miles and a real buyer's trust.

### Rebuilding the assistant

I took the running assistant and rebuilt it for the size of the promotion. Its invoice validation flow, which reads each purchase by OCR, was rebuilt to read any invoice a buyer sends, whatever the store or the format, and to pass what it reads to the checks.

### Rewriting the prompt

I rewrote the bot's prompt from the ground up. The new one follows the claim step by step, in the order the validation needs it, and keeps the conversation inside the promotion's rules, so the assistant explains what it can do instead of improvising what it cannot.

### Validation the model cannot talk its way around

The model is good at reading and at conversation. It should not be the one deciding whether a claim is valid, so the decisions moved to places a persuasive message cannot reach:

- **Deterministic IMEI validation.** Every IMEI goes through the Luhn check in code, and it is cross-checked between the data blocks of the claim. An IMEI that fails the check is rejected, whatever the conversation says.
- **A second, independent model for identity documents.** A single model's mistake cannot approve a claim on its own: a separate model verifies the identity document.
- **Promotion-validity blocking.** Purchases that fall outside the promotion's terms are blocked before any mile is credited.

### Messages built on verified data

What the buyer reads had to be as reliable as the checks behind it. The closing summary, the message where the buyer sees what was registered, is built only on verified data from the claim. And the assistant's internal working stays internal: its data blocks are filtered out before any message is sent, so customers only ever see the conversation.

### Closing the channel cleanly

A promotion ends, and its channel has to end well too. When it closed, every chat received a fixed notice once, and then the assistant went silent instead of answering claims it could no longer honour. I tested the closure on a real phone before switching it on.

The technical work was built with AI coding agents (Claude Code) under my direction. I led the campaign, rewrote the prompt, set the validation rules and the order of the work, and supervised the operation until the channel closed.

## Results

- More than **2 million LifeMiles miles** were awarded to Samsung buyers through the chatbot.
- The fraud controls (deterministic IMEI validation, a second model on identity documents and promotion-validity blocking) were **live before the promotion's peak**.
- Every closing summary was built on verified data, and customer messages carried nothing internal.
- The channel closed cleanly when the promotion ended, with one notice per chat and then silence.
- All of it on the same platform that serves Incentiva's other WhatsApp lines.

## What This Demonstrates

A promotion with real value is a security problem as much as a marketing one. An AI assistant that decides everything by itself is the easiest door for anyone trying to game it, and the most likely to turn an honest buyer away for no reason.

The design puts each decision where it belongs. The model reads invoices and talks to buyers, which is what it is good at. Deterministic code validates the IMEI, a second model checks identity, and the promotion's rules block what falls outside them. That is what let buyers claim their miles from their phone, and what let the chatbot award more than 2 million LifeMiles miles with confidence.

It is also what leading a campaign on your own platform looks like: the business rules, the prompt and the controls are decided by the same person, and AI agents build them, under that direction, in the order the promotion needs.
